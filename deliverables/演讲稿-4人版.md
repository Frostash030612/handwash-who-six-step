# PART A · Xu Wenzhe — 封面 · 动机 · 范围（1:40）

> **P1 封面**（35 秒）
> Good morning. We are Group 43: Shen Ziyi, Wang Lepeng, Zhu Jianyu, and I'm Xu Wenzhe. Our project is a video-based assessment of the WHO six-step handwashing technique. It recognises the steps from video and turns them into a report a person can check. Training runs in the cloud; inference runs on a local machine behind a camera web page. I'll cover the problem and scope, Ziyi the data, Lepeng the model and system, and Jianyu the demo, the report rules and our evaluation.

> **P2 动机**（27 秒）
> A useful review needs more than pass or fail. It should show which steps happened, when they happened, and which were missed. The difficulty is visual. Several steps look almost identical in a single image, the hands overlap, and real people skip, repeat or reorder steps. So the system has to keep those deviations visible, instead of forcing every wash into a fixed one-to-six sequence.

> **P3 项目范围**（38 秒）
> We aim to deliver two outputs. First, live recognition: the current step, plus a timeline with real timestamps. Second, a checkable report on coverage, order, duration, and repeated or missed steps. Our research question is whether a per-frame classifier stays reliable on a live camera feed, and whether its labels can become a report a user can verify. Our target is a Macro-F1 of at least 0.80 on held-out PSKUS videos. That is a goal, not a result. We will also measure end-to-end latency on the deployment machine. Ziyi, over to you.

# PART B · Shen Ziyi — 数据 · 质控（1:20）

> **过渡**：Thank you. I'm Shen Ziyi, and I'm responsible for data and evaluation.

> **P4 数据集**（35 秒）
> Our main source is PSKUS: 3,185 real hospital videos with frame-level labels from several annotators. That is the source total, not what we have processed. So far we use two of its eleven shards, DataSet1 and DataSet4: 153 videos that each have two independent annotations. For cross-domain testing we plan to use METC, 212 laboratory videos, which we have not downloaded yet. And each of us will record four to six clips, complete, missed, swapped and too-short washes, to test a real camera.

> **P5 数据质控**（43 秒）
> Reliable training starts with reliable labels, so we follow three rules. First, we split by source video, 70, 15 and 15 percent with a fixed seed. That gives 107 training, 23 validation and 23 test videos, and no video appears on both sides. Second, every frame we sample, at five per second, keeps its own label and real timestamp. Third, we handle action changes deliberately. The frame classifier only sees frames where both annotators agree and the label holds for about half a second. The temporal model trains on continuous 12- to 40-second windows and keeps disagreements as soft labels. Lepeng, over to you.

# PART C · Wang Lepeng — 架构 · 交互（1:25）

> **过渡**：Thanks, Ziyi. I'm Wang Lepeng, and I'm responsible for the model and training.

> **P6 系统架构**（48 秒）
> The system has two stages. First, in the cloud, we sample frames at five per second and fine-tune a pre-trained YOLO26 image classifier on the cleaned, video-disjoint splits. Second, we export the weights with their label order and preprocessing, check them on recorded video and the six-step report, and serve them locally behind the camera page.
> The slide shows our planned formal setting, the nano model at 224 pixels. Today's demo uses an earlier cloud run, exp.pt: the medium model at 320 pixels, trained on 3,250 consensus frames, with 85 percent top-1 on validation frames. That is an image-level check, not our Macro-F1 target.

> **P7 交互设计**（37 秒）
> This is what the user sees. They pick a camera, or load a local video for offline validation. During the session the page shows the current step, its confidence and the six-step progress. These live results are provisional, because they use only the frames received so far. When the user stops, the report is computed over the whole session. Frames are processed on the local machine, and the server keeps predictions and reports, not raw video. Camera accuracy and latency are not measured yet. Jianyu will show it running.

# PART D · Zhu Jianyu — 演示 · 报告 · 评估（2:30）

> **过渡**：Thanks, Lepeng. I'm Zhu Jianyu, and I'm responsible for the temporal model and the report rules.

> **P8 演示：实时识别与报告**（1:40）
> Here the page runs on a public PSKUS clip loaded as a local video, with exp.pt plus a GRU temporal head. On the left is the live view: it lists the time recognised for each step, five to eight seconds each, and at the end the hands have left the view, so it shows Other. *（指向左图右侧的六步时长）*
> Why add a temporal head? On held-out videos, one frame was often not enough. When the hands drift to the top edge of the view, the frame model read steps 3, 4 or 5 as step 6 for seconds at a time, and smoothing cannot undo a consistent error. The GRU reads the per-frame features in order, using only past frames, so it still works live.
> On the right is the report for the same clip: all six steps, in the correct order, and a score of 95. *（指向右图）* One issue is flagged: 37.2 seconds of rubbing, under the 40 seconds the WHO recommends, so the verdict is complete and in order, but too short.
> The rules are deterministic. A step counts once it adds up to one second. Order is checked on the six steps only, and a step interrupted by Other and then resumed is not a repeat. The score weights coverage at one half, order at 30 percent and duration at 20. This is model output, not verified compliance, and the score is not an accuracy measure.

> **P9 实验与评估**（50 秒）
> To judge whether it works, we compare four settings with the same data, splits, seed and backbone: A, frame-only; B, the same checkpoint with probability smoothing; C, a mean-pool head; and D, a GRU or TCN head.
> D already has a first result. On the 23 test videos, compared with the smoothed frame model, the GRU head raised agreement from 87 to 94 percent on frames where all annotators agree, and false order flags fell from two videos to one. That is preliminary, on two shards only. Next we will report per-class recall, the confusion matrix and Macro-F1, then missed-step precision and recall and segment F1 for the report, and test METC, our own recordings and latency. Wenzhe will close.

# 附：Q&A 应答卡（谁被问到谁答，括号内为数据出处）

**Q1「准确率到底多少？」**（C / D）
还没有正式结果，Macro-F1 没算出来。目前只有两个初步数字：
- exp.pt 在验证集上 top-1 为 **85.1%**。这是图像级指标，验证图只包含两位标注者一致且动作稳定的帧（470 张）。
- GRU 时序头在 **23 段 test 视频**、所有标注者一致的帧上，与标注的一致率从 **87% 提到 94%**；对照组是 exp.pt 加上过去 9 帧概率平均，也就是现在页面的做法。乱序误报从 2 段视频降到 1 段。

两个数字都只来自 DataSet1/4，不是摄像头实测。
*英文要点*：“No formal Macro-F1 yet. Two preliminary checks: 85 percent top-1 on consensus validation frames, and 87 to 94 percent frame agreement on 23 held-out videos with the temporal head. Both on two PSKUS shards, not on a real camera.”
（exp.pt 的 `train_metrics`；`exp_temporal_head.pt` 内保存的 `metrics`）

**Q2「PPT 写 8 类，演示模型为什么是 7 类？」**（C）
PSKUS 的动作编码是 0–7：other、六个步骤、关水龙头。exp.pt 把关水龙头并入 other，所以是 7 类。正式目标仍按 8 类计算 Macro-F1，六个步骤单独报告。

**Q3「为什么用整图分类，不做手部检测或关键点？」**（C）
PSKUS 只有逐帧的动作标签，没有手部框或关键点标注。分类加迁移学习是最直接的基线，推理开销小，适合放在本机部署（延迟还没测，不要说“实时已达标”）。更强的视觉编码器（PE）加 MS-TCN++ 列为候选方案，见 P9。

**Q4「标注者意见不一致怎么处理？」**（B）
分歧主要在“这一帧算某一步，还是算 other”。处理方式分三处：
- 帧分类器只用两人一致、并且前后约半秒标签不变的帧。
- 时序头把分歧当作软标签。例如两人标第 3 步、两人标 other，就按各占 0.5 训练。
- 评估逐帧一致率时，只统计所有标注者都一致的帧。

**Q5「为什么用 GRU，不用 Transformer？」**（D）
训练视频只有 107 段。GRU 很小（隐藏层 128），只看过去的帧，可以一帧一帧流式推理；主干网络冻结，只训练 GRU，几分钟就能完成。TCN 和 mean-pool 会在 P9 的 C/D 组里对比。

**Q6「这是一次正确的洗手，报告为什么说时长不足？」**（D）
时长是根据模型输出算的：手离开画面或动作不标准时会被判成 Other，不计入搓洗时间。时长误差（duration error）是 P9 列出的评估指标，还没有和人工标注对比过，所以不要把 37.2 秒当作真实时长。

**Q7「延迟多少？能实时吗？」**（C / A）
还没有测量。页面每秒送 5 帧。计划测量“采集到显示”的端到端延迟，以及每秒能处理的帧数（P3、P9）。

**Q8「隐私怎么保证？」**（A）
画面在本机浏览器采集，送到本机的推理服务。服务端只保存步骤预测和报告，不保存原始视频（页面底部有这句说明）。

**Q9「在真实摄像头上效果如何？」**（A / B）
还没有结果。下一步是每位组员自录 4–6 段（完整、漏步、换序、过短），再加上 METC 跨场景测试；真实摄像头验收是路线图的第 7 步。

**Q10「Other 指什么？」**（B）
不属于六步的动作，例如冲洗、关水龙头，以及手离开画面的时段。

**Q11「396 个测试是现在跑出来的吗？」**（A）
不是。396 是 CHANGELOG 里记录的契约层用例数，不是今天新跑的结果。CI 已经配置好，但远端最近一次的运行状态没有单独确认过。
